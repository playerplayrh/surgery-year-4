# Gastric and Small Bowel Injury
Source: Surgical Board Review 2 (2563), นพ.พงษ์สันติ์ ทองเนียม · printed pages 371-382 (PDF pages 2505-2516) · Trauma Edition 2 (chapter dated 2 มกราคม 2559)

- The stomach is a thick-walled vascularized organ in the upper abdomen; injury usually comes from penetrating wounds entering the upper abdomen or lower chest.
- The small bowel occupies most of the peritoneal cavity, so an abdominal GSW hits the small bowel or its mesentery in 80%; a stab wound hits the small bowel in 25-30%.

## GASTRIC INJURY
- **Mechanism of injury**
  1. Penetrating injury 99%
  2. Blunt trauma 1%
- **Diagnosis**: usually made at operation. Suspect when:
  1. Penetrating injury to the upper abdomen or lower chest
  2. Blunt trauma with bruising or abrasion on the abdomen, or seat belt sign
  3. Gastric perforation presents like perforated PU — rigid abdomen
  4. Blood on NG
  5. Film shows free air in about 50%; for higher sensitivity → CT
- Clear abdominal signs → no problem, straight to OR.
- The problem: brain or spinal cord injury, or a stable tangential GSW without abdominal signs → use adjuncts such as CT, DPL or laparoscopy.

## Surgical Management
- Midline incision.
- Hole seen → temporarily close with a Babcock clamp; wash out food debris first (as printed, a list of Thai dishes: rice porridge, stir-fried bean sprouts, morning glory, Chinese kale with salted fish, dried-shrimp salad).
- Mobilize the stomach gently — do not pull so hard that the spleen tears.
- Explore the whole stomach carefully, like examining a pretty model at a motor show (as printed): look for bruises and hematoma on both lesser and greater curves.
- Beware missed injury near the GE junction, upper fundus and upper lesser curve; dividing the left triangular ligament improves the view.
- **Always explore the posterior wall** (ต้องสำรวจทาง posterior wall เสมอ): open the gastrocolic ligament along the greater curve outside the gastroepiploic arcade. Caption: the posterior wall of the stomach and anterior surface of the pancreas can be approached by dividing the gastrocolic ligament and lifting the stomach superiorly.
- Very small hole, or a second hole cannot be found → fill the abdomen with water and have the anaesthetist pump air; a perforation will bubble.
- Intramural hematoma → open the seromuscular layer, scoop out clot, stop bleeding, then close with silk 3-0.
- Perforation → debride, then close in 2 layers.
- Wound near the GE junction → suture carefully; too-large bites can narrow the lumen.
- Wound at the pylorus → if closure looks likely to narrow it, do pyloroplasty.
- Gastric resection only for very severe injury or devascularization of the stomach.

## Gastric Injury Associated With Diaphragmatic Injury
- Problem: contamination of the pleural cavity by gastric content.
- Minor contamination → enlarge the diaphragm wound a little, wash the pleural space through it until clean, close the diaphragm and insert a chest drain.
- Severe contamination ("severve" as printed) or a delayed case → add a thoracotomy, wash clean, leave a chest tube. Thoracoscopic evacuation is an alternative option.

## SMALL BOWEL INJURY
- **Mechanism of injury**: penetrating more often than blunt trauma.
- Blunt trauma causes small bowel injury by:
  1. Sudden deceleration — shearing force at fixed points, e.g. IC valve, ligament of Treitz, or adhesion sites
  2. Crush against the vertebral body
  3. Blow-out of a fluid-filled loop
- **Diagnosis**: penetrating injury → perforation usually found at laparotomy. Blunt trauma is harder: contusion may cause no symptoms; even a perforation may cause few symptoms early because spilled small-bowel secretion is still neutral and relatively sterile, until bacteria enter and peritonitis develops.
- Especially in blunt trauma, think of small bowel injury ("small injury" as printed) when there is:
  1. Transverse fracture of the L3 body = **Chance fracture** — associated small bowel injury in up to 50%
  2. Seat belt sign
- So in blunt trauma, the best way to diagnose small bowel injury in a conscious, sensible patient is abdominal examination — better than any film, which usually shows only thickened bowel wall. The CT we like to order usually shows free fluid and Chance fracture. DPL may yield green bile-stained fluid with food debris, but it is not necessary — many false negatives, and noninvasive CT already gives enough information.
- Blunt abdominal trauma + seat belt sign + CT free fluid and Chance fracture → risk of small bowel injury is so high; recommendation: take to OR for explore lap.

## Small Bowel Injury Scale

| Grade | Type of injury | Description of injury |
|---|---|---|
| I | Hematoma | Contusion or hematoma without devascularization |
| I | Laceration | Partial thickness, no perforation |
| II | Laceration | Laceration < 50% of circumference |
| III | Laceration | Laceration ≥ 50% of circumference without transection ("fransection" as printed) |
| IV | Laceration | Transection of the small bowel |
| V | Laceration | Transection of the small bowel with segmental tissue loss ("fissue" as printed) |
| V | Vascular | Devascularized segment |

- Advance one grade for multiple injuries up to grade III.

## Surgical Management
- On opening, deal first with life-threatening injury problems.
- Small bowel: examine carefully from the ligament of Treitz to the IC valve ("IC value" as printed), turning front and back. Holes → clamp with Babcock. Perforations should always come in pairs; an odd number means search again — one may be hidden near the mesenteric border.
- **Managing perforations**
  - Caption: grade I → inversion with seromuscular sutures; grade II → careful debridement and primary closure, one or two layers; adjacent through-and-through perforations are treated as a single defect by dividing the bridge of tissue between them with electrocautery.
  - Summary (สรุป): grade 1, 2 → primary repair; grade 3, 4, 5 → resect and anastomose.
  1. Debride and close the hole in 2 layers transversely.
  2. Two adjacent holes → debride into a single hole, then close transversely.
  3. Multiple perforations close together — debriding and closing each would take long, or high-velocity war weapon injury → resection-anastomosis works better.
  4. Hole > 50% of circumference → resection-anastomosis is better.
  - Caption: grade III injuries are usually treated by resection and anastomosis; proximal small bowel injuries or transversely oriented wounds may occasionally be primarily repaired.
- **Summary — indications for resection with anastomosis** (สรุป)
  1. Full-thickness tear > 50% of the circumference
  2. Expanding or mesenteric injury with bowel ischemia
  3. Multiple injury in a short segment
- **Mesenteric hematoma** — explore if:
  1. Large hematoma (in theory, size > 2 cm warrants opening)
  2. Expanding hematoma
  3. Hematoma near the root of the mesentery
- Debated problem: intramural hematoma of the jejunum or ileum found at OR. The author follows Trunkey's Current Therapy of Trauma:
  1. Limited in extension and nonexpanding → leave alone; it will absorb.
  2. Large or expanding → open the hematoma, scoop out clot, stop bleeding, then check bowel colour; if it looks bad (dark, dusky), resect and anastomose.
- Last point: whether sutured or anastomosed, do it well — no leaks. Green bile draining from the drain on the morning round after surgery feels like falling into hell (เหมือนตกนรก).
