## Introduction
- Pediatric surgery is a field that stresses surgical residents (as printed, a long joke about small children who cry, cannot explain themselves, and need patience and soothing).
- Pediatric surgery questions in the exam: no more than 5 items per year; with good basics you can answer almost all of them.
- In real life, what a general surgery resident should know is mainly emergency diseases/conditions in children; operations to do yourself are e.g. appendectomy, hernia, circumcision (ขริบจู๋), cyst excision. Hard, complex cases such as anomalies (no anus, anus not open) → do not get involved; refer.
- The text is a short note of what a general surgeon should know, kept simple, aimed at answering exam questions.
- Acknowledgements (as printed): พ.ญ.วรางคณา ธรรมสัญญา checked the original draft; อาจารย์ ชาญวิทย์ วัฒนสานติ์ (pediatric surgeon, รพ.พระมงกุฎเกล้า) corrected the content.

## General Considerations
- Use basic knowledge to handle the children's questions well enough to answer the examiner (as printed, joke).
- Premature (as printed): baby born before 37 wk.
- Low birth weight: birth weight < 2,500 gm.
- Immunology: from birth, IgA comes from the mother; after 1 year, IgM.
- Heart rate as shock indicator: neonate HR > 150, or age < 1 year HR > 120 → suspect shock.
- Urine output in children should be 1-4 cc/kg/hr with specific gravity 1.005-1.010; urine < 1 cc/kg/hr in a child is too little — not good.
- Umbilical vessels: 2 arteries and 1 vein.
- High alkaline phosphatase in children is usually normal, from bone growth; in adults a high ALP suggests bile duct obstruction.

## Fluid & Electrolyte
- **Maintenance fluid** — Holliday and Segar rule:
  1. First 10 kg: 100 cc/kg
  2. Next 10-20 kg: 50 cc/kg
  3. > 20 kg: 20 cc/kg
- **Urine output**
  1. Infant 1-2 cc/kg/hr
  2. Older child 0.5-1 cc/kg/hr
- **Sodium**: 2-3 mEq/kg/day, then work out whether to give as N/2, N/3 or N/4; in critical illness may need up to 5 mEq/kg/day.
- **Potassium**: 1-2 mEq/kg/day.
- **Blood replacement**
  1. PRC 10 cc/kg
  2. FFP 20 cc/kg
  3. Platelet 1 unit per 5 kg body weight
- Adequacy of nutrition: look at daily weight gain.

## VACTERL Anomalies
- Anomalies commonly found with esophageal atresia & TE fistula and imperforate anus.
- Positive in 3/6 items → true VACTERL anomalies.
  1. V — Vertebral anomalies, usually LS spine, not C spine
  2. A — Anorectal malformation, especially imperforate anus
  3. C — Cardiac defect
  4. TE — Tracheo-esophageal fistula & esophageal atresia
  5. R — Renal anomalies
  6. L — Limb anomalies

## Extracorporeal Life Support (ECLS)
- Cardiopulmonary bypass used as temporary support for critically ill patients with acute refractory respiratory or cardiac failure: desaturated venous blood is drained through extracorporeal membrane oxygenation (ECMO), which removes CO2 and adds O2; the oxygenated warm blood returns to the patient via venoarterial bypass (cannulation of internal jugular vein and common carotid artery).
- **Indications for ECMO**
  1. Meconium aspiration
  2. Respiratory distress syndrome
  3. PPHN
  4. Sepsis
  5. Congenital diaphragmatic hernia

## Neck
- Thyroglossal duct cyst, branchial cleft cyst and cystic hygroma → see Chapter Head and Neck.

## Torticollis
- Wry neck from fibrosis of the sternocleidomastoid muscle → the muscle shortens and pulls the neck to tilt; the face turns permanently to the opposite side.
- Diagnosis is clinical in about 70% — palpable mass in the muscle; biopsy shows deposition of collagen and fibroblasts around atrophied muscle cells.
- **Treatment**
  1. Physical therapy, passive stretching of the muscle → 80-90% improve in 6-12 months
  2. If not resolved after 1 year of PT → transverse division of the sternal and clavicular heads of SCM (division of sternocleidomastoid muscle)

## Respiratory System
- Residents should know 2 topics: Bochdalek hernia and foreign bodies.

## Congenital Diaphragmatic or Bochdalek Hernia
- In the fetus the chest and abdominal cavities are initially not separated; the diaphragm forms later to divide them, and the last part to close is the posterolateral area. If it fails to close → Bochdalek hernia.
- Figure: Diaphragm defects — parasternal (Morgagni) hernia anteriorly; posterolateral (Bochdalek) hernia; agenesis of diaphragm (septum transversum defect).
- Incidence 1:5,000; F:M = 2:1; 80% on the left.
- 80% have other anomalies, e.g. heart and bowel; most important is a large defect, so the lungs fail to grow because the bowel compresses them.
- When both lungs fail to develop (aplasia) or develop incompletely (severe pulmonary hypoplasia), with abdominal organs herniating up to compress lung tissue, pulmonary vascular resistance exceeds systemic vascular resistance → pulmonary hypertension, the key dominant factor for survival. In summary, prognosis and mortality relate to the degree of pulmonary hypoplasia, and death is from pulmonary HT.
- **Symptoms** (อาการ) — may present at birth or 2-3 days after delivery:
  1. Respiratory distress from pulmonary hypoplasia; cyanosis
  2. Scaphoid abdomen — flat abdomen because the bowel has moved into the chest
  3. Mediastinum shifts to the opposite side
  4. Heart sounds heard on the right
- **Diagnosis** (การวินิจฉัยโรค)
  1. US — prenatal US; besides checking the baby's sex, scan the chest too; if bowel is still in the chest after 5 months, beware Bochdalek hernia
  2. Chest x-ray — no aeration of the left lung, cardiac shadow shifted to the right; if bowel is seen in the chest, no need to confirm with contrast study
  3. ABG — hypoxia, hypercarbia and respiratory acidosis
- **Treatment** (การรักษา)
  1. Dyspnea or cyanosis → promptly intubate (ET tube) and connect to high-frequency ventilator; do not use a face mask — it distends the stomach and bowel further
  2. Pulmonary HT with untreatable cardiorespiratory failure → may need extracorporeal membrane oxygenation (ECMO); ECMO needs heparin, so there must be no bleeding problem such as intracranial hemorrhage
  3. NG tube to decompress the stomach. Newborns breathe through the nose and the nostrils are small, so it often has to go through the mouth; do not forget IV + sodium bicarb
  4. Stabilize: warming, avoid barotrauma and hypoxia, then operate when the patient is ready — safer; emergency surgery on a poorly prepared baby often ends in death
  5. Laparotomy via subcostal incision, pull the bowel down, repair the diaphragm defect: small hole → suture closed; large hole → mesh. Primary repair → survival about 95%; synthetic patch repair needed (large defect) → survival 79%
  6. CVT surgeons experienced with scopes have started thoracoscopic repair, also successful, but it takes longer and recurrence is higher than open repair
- Intrauterine repair and tracheal occlusion are now done; how is a matter for those who want to train further in pediatric surgery (as printed).

## Foreign Bodies (FB)
- This chapter covers only FB in the respiratory system; FB in the GI tract is in the GI chapter.
- Airway FB in young children is usually food (e.g. peanuts, sunflower seeds), common in the toddler stage when they are curious and put things in the mouth; older children usually inhale school items such as pen caps.
- 50% have a history of choking during a meal → suspect FB aspiration.
- In 14-45% physical exam is normal and diagnostic imaging (plain film or CT) finds nothing; these are the cases that cause doctors trouble.
- Classify airway FB by location:
  1. **Upper FB** — lodged from oropharynx and larynx down to trachea: the child coughs, chokes, airway obstruction with wheezing, stridor, cyanosis → urgent treatment.
     - 1.1 Small child, life saving: hold the child prone, head down, and slap the back between both scapulae with the heel of the hand 5 times; if the FB has not come out, turn the child supine and press the sternum with 2 fingers 5 times. Older child: if conscious → Heimlich; if unconscious → chest compression.
     - 1.2 If the FB still does not come out → push a size 14 needle through the cricothyroid membrane to solve the problem first, then definitive treatment.
  2. **Lower FB** — from the bronchial bifurcation down; usually lodges in the RLL.
     - Symptoms (อาการ): small FB may cause no early symptoms; later recurrent pneumonia; wheeze or crepitations on auscultation.
     - Chest x-ray: inspiration and expiration views; in expiration or on fluoroscopy the obstructed lung shows hyperlucency (hyperaeration) and does not deflate; pneumonia or atelectasis may be seen.
     - Bronchoscope: best tool for both diagnosis and treatment; rigid bronchoscopy under GA is better than flexible bronchoscopy with conscious sedation — the large lumen assesses well and pulls the FB out more easily.
     - Treatment (การรักษา): whether or not the x-ray shows it, a suspicious history → bronchoscopy; if FB is found, remove it. Do not leave it — observation leads to infection and bronchiectasis later.

## Esophageal Atresia & Tracheoesophageal Fistula
- Esophagus and trachea start as one tube and separate at about 5 weeks' gestation; abnormal separation causes this disease.
- Incidence 1:3,000; F > M slightly (as printed); associated with VACTERL anomalies.
- 6 types; exam favourites are A and C.

| Type | Description | Frequency |
|---|---|---|
| A | Upper blind, lower blind | 2nd most common, about 5-10% |
| B | Upper connected (fistula), lower blind | 2% |
| C | Upper blind, lower connected | Most common, about 85% |
| D | Upper and lower both connected | 1% |
| E | H-type fistula only, no atresia | 4% |
| F | Esophageal stenosis without fistula | Very rare (โคตร rare) |

- **Symptoms** (อาการ): drooling of sticky saliva, frothing at the mouth, vomiting from birth, choking at feeds.
  - Type A: flat, scaphoid abdomen; abdominal x-ray shows no gas in the GI tract at all.
  - Type C: lower end connected, so air enters the stomach → distended abdomen. The serious complication in this type is reflux of gastric content through the lower TE fistula into the lung.
- Do not forget to rule out VACTERAL (as printed) anomalies; about 35% have cardiac anomalies, often the cause of death.
- **Diagnosis**
  1. Suspected case → try passing an NG tube size 8 through the mouth; in true disease it goes in no more than 10-13 cm and sticks. Chest x-ray shows the NG tube coiled in the upper esophagus at T2.
  2. Prenatal US can diagnose it in utero: suspect when no gas is seen in the stomach, the mother has polyhydramios (as printed), and there is a dilated upper esophageal pouch.
- **Treatment** (การรักษา)
  1. NPO, IV, OG tube or sump/double-lumen catheter on continuous low-pressure suction of the upper esophagus
  2. Head-up position (semi-flower position or head-up prone position) 30-45° to prevent regurgitation of gastric content
  3. Treat pneumonia with antibiotic, give vitamin K; routine ET intubation should be avoided
  4. Timing of surgery depends mainly on the baby's condition, using Spitz's classification:

| Grade | Birth weight | Cardiac anomalies | Survival (%) |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | > 1,500 gm | No | 97 |
| 2 | < 1,500 gm | ± | 59 |
| 3 | < 1,500 gm | Yes | 22 |

  - 4.1 Good condition (สภาพดี), able to tolerate major surgery → right thoracotomy; divide the azygos vein; the fistula lies under the vein; divide it, anastomose the esophagus and close the fistula as one stage.
  - 4.2 Type A with a gap > 2 vertebrae → gastrostomy first, wait about 3-6 months for the esophagus to lengthen, or try periodic bougienage tube dilation to stretch both esophageal ends, then join them; if they still cannot reach or tension is high, use stomach or colon as conduit.
  - 4.3 Type C → close the fistula and anastomose the esophagus.
  - 4.3 (numbered as printed) Baby too unwell (สภาพเด็กไม่ไหว), e.g. very small premature, Spitz grade 3, serious VACTERL, or upper and lower esophagus > 6 vertebrae apart and cannot be pulled together → cervical esophagostomy to drain saliva plus gastrostomy for feeding first; when the baby is older than 3 months, reconstruct later with colon interposition, gastric pull-up or reverse gastric tube.

## Gastrointestinal Tract
- Only diseases a surgical resident should know are selected.

## Tongue Tie (Ankyloglossia)
- Born with a fibrous band tethering the underside of the tongue → the tongue cannot help suck milk; the baby uses the gums to grip the nipple until the mother's nipple is sore and peels → mastitis.
- Treatment: cut the band (frenulotomy).

## Infantile Hypertrophic Pyloric Stenosis (HPS)
- Incidence 1:300; cause unknown why the pyloric circular muscle thickens until the pyloric channel narrows. Familial pattern; usually the first child and male; M:F = 4:1.
- Usual age at diagnosis 2-6 weeks. The baby feeds for 15-20 minutes and then has projectile vomiting; key point is non-bilious vomiting. The baby becomes very dehydrated with hypochloremic hypokalemic metabolic alkalosis from paradoxical aciduria.
- PE: visible peristalsis may be seen; in 70-90% a 2 cm olive-like mass (olive mass) is palpable in the RUQ.
- US is now the investigation of choice: pyloric muscle thickness > 4 mm and length > 16 mm, or on transverse section the thick muscle gives a Bull's eye sign; US accuracy > 98%.
- UGI, popular in the past, is now out of fashion — the baby may vomit and aspirate into the lung. UGI findings: dilated stomach (dilated gastric bubble) and a narrow, thin pyloric canal like a thread (string sign) — not coil spring sign.
- **Treatment** (การรักษา) — prepare the baby well first:
  1. Correct fluid & electrolyte: IV 5% D/N/2 first; add KCl once urine flows
  2. NG tube to decompress the stomach
  3. Surgery is elective when the patient is ready; laparoscopy is now popular — faster return to full feeding and fewer wound infections than open. Incise the seromuscular layer of the pylorus lengthwise until mucosa & submucosa bulge out, like a Heller in achalasia. This is the Fredet Ramstedt pyloromyotomy, the procedure of choice.
- After surgery, feeding starts within 24 h; there may be vomiting after 1-2 feeds, which usually settles; most go home 2-3 days after surgery.

## Duodenal Atresia
- Most common cause of duodenal obstruction in newborns < 1 year.
- The foregut develops from 2-3 months of gestation as a solid core, which later vacuolizes into a hollow tube. Failure of recanalization of the duodenum → stenosis or atresia. The obstruction is usually distal to the ampulla of Vater (85%), so the vomitus contains bile.
- Vomiting usually starts after birth (about 2-3 days); 1/3 of cases have Down syndrome.
- Must exclude other causes of duodenal obstruction: malrotation and midgut volvulus, duodenal web, duodenal duplication cyst, annular pancreas.
- 2/3 of cases are premature labor; 50% have polyhydramios (as printed) because the baby cannot swallow amniotic fluid; VACTERL anomalies in up to 52%. So even with antenatal detection survival is not better, especially with congenital heart disease. Prenatal US showing polyhydramnios and double bubble sign → suspect this disease.
- Film abdomen: double bubble sign — gas only in the stomach and proximal duodenum, no gas in the distal small bowel; this typical finding needs no further confirmation.
- The disease's features are "3D": Duodenal atresia, Down syndrome and Double bubble sign.
- **Treatment** (การรักษา): not urgent; correct fluid & electrolyte first, operate when the baby is ready. 1st choice duodenoduodenostomy (diamond-shape duodenoduodenostomy); if difficult, duodenojejunostomy instead — acceptable. Gastrojejunostomy — pediatric surgeons dislike it, so do not choose it.

## Jejuno-Ileal Atresia
- Believed to come from loss of blood supply to the bowel in utero (intrauterine vascular accident); the exact mechanism — never mind (as printed).
- Associated with polyhydramios (as printed) 30%; VACTERL associated in < 10%.
- On polyhydramnios: normally the fetus swallows amniotic fluid, which is absorbed in the jejunum and ileum; if the bowel is narrowed or blocked, fluid cannot be swallowed down → polyhydramnios in the mother.
- Beware multiple locations — atresia at more than 1 site in 20-35%.
- Symptoms (อาการ): abdominal distension, no meconium passed, bilious vomiting 1-2 days after birth.
- Plain film abdomen: proximal small bowel larger than the thumb (rule of thumb), with air-fluid levels; bubbles form a triple bubble or multiple bubble sign; no colonic air.
- When atresia is low and the abdomen is very distended → BE to rule out Hirschsprung. BE in jejunoileal atresia shows a very small colon (no stool has passed through it).
- **Treatment** (การรักษา): when the baby is ready, go to OR, laparotomy, resect the atretic segment and anastomose. The problem is that proximal and distal bowel differ greatly in size; pediatric surgeons have special anastomotic techniques for this — end to back, tapering, or Bishop-Koop. Residents who want to know how should read a pediatric surgery textbook.

## Malrotation & Midgut Volvulus
- Very important (โคตรสำคัญ), fairly urgent, especially with volvulus; late diagnosis or late surgery → nearly all bowel supplied by the SMA is lost; if the baby survives, short bowel.
- Think of it in a child with bilious vomiting in the first week of life up to 4 weeks (not over 1 year), with abdominal distension, bilious vomiting, bloody mucoid stool, palpable mass in the mid abdomen.
- M = F; incidence 1:6000; 75% in the neonatal period, another 25% in infancy.
- **Basic development of the bowel**
  - At about 6 weeks in utero the bowel lies outside the abdominal cavity; by week 12 it returns into the abdomen, rotating counterclockwise 270° around the SMA. The duodenum (upper part) rotates into a C-loop and the superior mesenteric vessels lie anterior to the third part of the duodenum, while the cecum rotates to the right lower quadrant.
  - If the duodenum and cecum are tethered by Ladd band so they cannot rotate normally — only 180° — this is malrotation: the whole proximal small bowel stays on the right, and the cecum stays at the RUQ instead of the RLQ.
  - With the DJ position also running straight down on the right without crossing the midline, the small bowel mesentery root is narrow; peristalsis swings the bowel back and forth → twisting, or midgut volvulus: twisting around the mesentery causes SMA occlusion. Left untreated, 2 outcomes: death or short bowel.
  - Note (หมายเหตุ): if this part is confusing, accept it — basic embryology was returned to the preclinic teachers long ago (as printed).
- **Most classic symptoms** (อาการที่สุด classic)
  1. Early feeding and stooling normal (จำ)
  2. First symptom: feeds not tolerated, bilious vomiting from obstruction, no stool, abdominal distension in the first 3-7 days. Untreated → bowel ischemia, bloody mucoid stool, peritonitis, gangrenous bowel.
- Most serious complication: volvulus and bowel gangrene.
- Plain abdomen: not very specific, only small bowel dilatation. US showing SMA/SMV reversal has a high false positive rate.
- **Upper GI series**: best way to diagnose — incomplete rotation with small bowel on the right side of the abdomen; the DJ junction does not cross the midline; contrast runs into the jejunum straight down on the right; cork-screw appearance. Used in suspected cases at an early stage when the abdominal wall is not yet red.
- US: SMV lies left of the SMA (normally SMV is to the right of the SMA).
- **Treatment** (การรักษา)
  1. An emergency. A baby with bilious vomiting, irritability, abdominal pain, reddish abdominal wall → plain abdomen showing dilated proximal small bowel with air-fluid levels is enough; no need to confirm with upper GI series — by then the bowel is near gangrene, shock and death come soon. Explore promptly and do Ladd's procedure: reduce the volvulus, divide Ladd's band, arrange the mesentery, pull the colon to lie in the LUQ to widen the mesenteric root and separate the parts so twisting cannot recur. This puts the appendix in the wrong place, so remove the appendix to avoid future confusion.
     - Figure: Ladd procedure — A, bowel eviscerated; B, intestine derotated counterclockwise; C, peritoneal attachment between cecum and retroperitoneum (Ladd's band) divided; D, base of the mesentery widened and appendectomy performed.
  2. Late surgery with already gangrenous bowel → resect all gangrenous parts; tendency to short bowel for sure.
  3. Remember: a newborn with bilious vomiting in the 1st week → always rule out this disease.

## Meconium Ileus (Meconium Plug Syndrome)
- Common in Western newborns, not seen in Thai children, but you must know it because it is examined.
- Not truly ileus but a distal obstruction at the terminal ileum from a thick, sticky, hard meconium mass blocking it like constipation; pediatric surgeons call it meconium plug syndrome.
- 16% occur in children with cystic fibrosis (จำ); details on how cystic fibrosis makes meconium sticky with high sweat chloride are not needed for surgical residents.
- Symptoms (อาการ): SBO, e.g. abdominal distension, bilious vomiting.
- **Film abdomen**
  1. Small bowel dilatation while the colon is small (disuse colon)
  2. Air bubbles within the meconium mass in the RLQ — ground glass pattern, soap suds pattern or soap bubble
  3. Calcification on the mass surface like an eggshell — eggshell pattern
- **Treatment** (การรักษา)
  1. No perforation → conservative first: rectal irrigation with gastrograffin saline enema under fluoroscopy. Gastrograffin draws water into the bowel lumen, softening the meconium until it passes. Gastrograffin enema works in 80%.
  2. Enema fails or perforation → surgery to remove the obstructing meconium; resect and anastomose bowel if needed.
- **Remember** (ควรจำ)
  1. Conservative treatment works in up to 80%; not every case needs surgery
  2. Gastrograffin is given per rectum, not by mouth (จำ)

## Necrotizing Enterocolitis (NEC)
- Severe inflammation of the bowel that may progress to gangrene.
- **Risk factors**
  1. Premature labor — most strongly related; premature babies have immature bowel: lacking IgA, immature villi, poor or incomplete peristalsis compared with full-term babies
  2. Bacterial infection & sepsis
  3. Hypoalbuminemia
  4. Mesenteric ischemia
  5. Feeding too much or too fast; highly concentrated milk can irritate the mucosa
  6. Others, e.g. anemia, hypoxia, shock, even polycythemia
- With risk factors, bowel hypoperfusion → mucosal ischemia → bacterial invasion → severe infection.
- **Classic presentation**: premature, low birth weight infant with bloody mucoid, foul stool after the 1st feeding, abdominal distension, poor feeding, with NEC risk factors; then refuses feeds, abdominal pain, distension, vomiting, fever, lethargy; if the bowel perforates or becomes gangrenous, usually does not survive.
- Signs of gangrenous bowel: clinical deterioration, respiratory decompensation, bloody, very foul stool, swollen red abdominal wall, acidosis followed by DIC.
- **Plain abdomen**
  1. Early: ileus pattern, distended gassy bowel
  2. More bowel dilatation, pneumatosis intestinalis (ลูกศรชี้, arrows in the figure), portal vein gas, fixed loop that does not move → bad: high chance of gangrene and perforation, waiting to die
  3. Finally pneumoperitoneum
- **Treatment** (การรักษา)
  1. No sign of perforation → aggressive medical treatment: NPO, NG tube, IV, IV antibiotic with nutritional support (TPN), maintain oxygenation, close observation, serial film abdomen; about 80% improve.
  2. Worsening within 24-48 hr — progressive abdominal wall cellulitis, GI bleeding, fixed mass, diffuse peritonitis, septic shock, or free air on x-ray (signs of gangrene) → laparotomy, resect gangrenous bowel; mostly not anastomosed but brought out as multiple ostomies first. If viability is uncertain and much bowel would be removed → close the abdomen first and do a 2nd look in 24-48 hours. NEC needing bowel resection often leads to short bowel syndrome long term.

## Intussusception
- Incidence 1.5:1000; M > F slightly; age 5-36 months.
- The swallowed (inner) part is the intussusceptum, proximal; the outer part is the intussuscipiens (as printed "intussuscepiens"), distal. About 95% are ileocolic intussusception.
- **Leading point**: in children aged 5-36 months usually no cause. Exception: under 6 months, 2-12% may have Peyer's patch hypertrophy at the terminal ileum after viral infection (URI or GI) as the leading point. In children > 2 years, 2-12% also have a cause — most often Meckel, then lymphoma and intestinal polyp.
- **Symptoms** (อาการ)
  1. Sudden colicky abdominal pain — the child screams intermittently; abdominal distension, vomiting
  2. Classic stool: currant jelly stool (65-90%) — bloody mucoid stool
- **Physical exam** (ตรวจร่างกาย)
  1. Palpable mass in the RUQ — sausage mass (85-90%)
  2. RLQ feels empty — Dance's sign (as printed)
  3. PR may feel the mass; bloody mucoid stool on the glove
- **Investigation**
  1. US: transverse section shows donut or target sign; longitudinal section shows a kidney-like mass (pseudo kidney sign). US is nearly 100% accurate; no need to confirm with CT.
  2. BE: filling defect, a rounded mass in the bowel (crescent sign, printed "cresent"); barium seeps between the bowel layers of the swallowed segment like a spring = coilspring sign. No longer used now because US works very well.
- **Treatment** (การรักษา): most important is to start by correcting fluid and electrolytes, then:
  1. **Reduction** — both diagnostic and therapeutic. Pneumatic (air) reduction success > 90%, while hydrostatic reduction is lower, about 60%. Air enema pressure not over 100 mmHg. When reduction succeeds, pain resolves, air is seen refluxing into the small bowel; then repeat US to confirm the target sign is gone; if all is well, no surgery needed.
  2. **Surgery** — indications:
     - 2.1 Peritonitis, gangrenous bowel, or free air
     - 2.2 Failed reduction or multiple recurrence
     - 2.3 Evidence of a pathological lesion as leading point
- Operation: RLQ incision, gently mobilize the intussuscepted bowel onto the abdominal wall. If not gangrenous → intraoperative reduction by milking gently from distal to proximal, pushing from colon toward ileum until the swallowed bowel comes out. Do not pull the small bowel out of the large bowel — the strangled small bowel is friable and tears (as printed, the author has perforated it before). Before closing, remove the appendix too, since it is often ischemic.
- If the bowel is already gangrenous and foul → do not reduce it (reperfusion injury); or if milking fails to free it → right half colectomy, usually with primary anastomosis; then pray (as printed).
- Figure: Intussusception algorithm, as steps —
  1. Ultrasonography negative → observe for 24 hrs.
  2. Positive → ileoileal intussusception / definite lead point present? — Present → laparotomy. Absent → hydrostatic reduction.
  3. Hydrostatic reduction successful → 24 hours observation; not successful → laparotomy.
- Current practice (ปัจจุบัน): air reduction is used because it has fewer complications than hydrostatic reduction.

## Hirschsprung's Disease (HD)
- Incidence 1:5,000; M:F = 4:1, but in total colonic involvement F > M.
- Most common colon obstruction in infants, especially boys; most often rectum and rectosigmoid; transverse colon uncommon.
- About 80% diagnosed as newborns, 20% when older.
- **Cause** (สาเหตุ): absent ganglion cells in Auerbach plexus — neural crest cells that become ganglion cells fail to migrate down from proximal to distal bowel and stop at some point (normally they migrate from the esophagus to the rectum) → that bowel segment has no peristalsis.
- The aganglionic segment is usually in the left colon; > 80% lie below the sigmoid down to the rectum; right colon very uncommon.
- **Clinical manifestations**: the aganglionic segment is narrow with no peristalsis; the dilated part is the normal bowel. The child presents with functional large bowel obstruction: marked distension, vomiting. Clue: no meconium within 24 hours after birth; PR shows empty rectum; after an enema stool comes out and the child improves; then chronic constipation; without enemas no stool — a fecal impaction mass may be felt in the abdomen. Stool retained long → bacterial overgrowth, foul stool, high fever, distension, diarrhea, then enterocolitis. Now the stool is liquid and bloody — beware during PR: pulling the finger out releases foul, mud-coloured stool in a jet onto the doctor's face (as printed). Remember: most serious complication of Hirschsprung = enterocolitis.
- Meconium consists of amniotic fluid, debris, mucus; dark coloured; a normal baby passes meconium within 24 hours.
- **Investigation**
  1. **Suction rectal biopsy** — fairly specific diagnostic investigation, no anesthesia needed: suction at least 2 cm above the dentate line; usually gets only mucosa and submucosa, stained by immunohistochemistry for acetylcholinesterase enzyme; in HD, brown-stained cholinesterase is increased in the submucosa (immunohistochemical diagnosis). To look for ganglion cells, full thickness biopsy under general anesthesia is the gold standard: in HD no ganglion cells in the myenteric plexus and submucosal plexus (Meissner's plexus), or only hypertrophied nerve trunks.
  2. **Anorectal manometry** — measures pressure change in colon and rectum; in normal children raising rectal pressure relaxes the external and internal sphincters. In Hirschsprung there is no relaxation reflex — the anus does not relax with rectal distension. Suits older children; not popular now.
  3. **Imaging study**
     - 3.1 Plain abdomen: low gut obstruction — distended gassy large bowel, no air in the rectum
     - 3.2 BE: dilated proximal colon, distal spastic or narrow segment; the junction between dilated and narrow parts = transitional zone. Rectal biopsies are diagnostic and show lack of ganglion cells.
- **Distinguishing HD from functional fecal retention**

| Symptom/sign | Functional fecal retention | Hirschsprung's disease |
|---|---|---|
| Constipation as a newborn | Rarely | Almost always |
| Soiling | Common | Almost never |
| Difficult toilet training | Common | Rare |
| Avoidance of toilet | Common | Rare |
| Withholding behavior | Common | Rare |
| Stool in rectal vault | Common | Rare |
| Obstructive symptoms | Rare | Common |
| Barium enema | Transition zone absent | Transition zone often present |
| Anorectal manometry | Internal sphincter relaxation present | Internal sphincter relaxation absent |
| Rectal biopsy pathology | Ganglia present | Ganglia absent |

- **Treatment** (การรักษา): every HD case needs surgery. First correct fluid and electrolytes and wash out stool, e.g. normal saline irrigation, or colostomy in febrile cases with very foul bloody stool (suggesting enterocolitis); at this step give antibiotics, until the child is heavy enough for surgery.
  1. In the past, small infants had two stages: colostomy first, wait until 10 kg, then pull-through operation. Now it can be done from the newborn period, usually one stage: resect colon up to proximal colon with ganglion cells and anastomose to the anus as a pull-through, no prior colostomy.
  2. Diagnosed in an older child, with very different bowel calibre → loop colostomy first to buy time; wait 3-6 months for the bowel to shrink, then pull-through.
- **Transabdominal pull-through operations** — 3 types; knowing the names is enough:
  1. Duhamel (recto-colectomy pull-through)
  2. Swenson (recto-rectal pull-through)
  3. Soave (endo-rectal pull-through)
  (names and descriptions paired as printed)
- Trans-anal pull-through: done entirely through the anus; suits children with a low transitional zone.
- Laparoscopic surgery is increasingly used now.

## Anorectal Malformation
- Congenital abnormality: the child is born without an anal opening.
- Incidence 1:5,000; M = F; associated with VACTERAL (as printed) anomalies 30-40%.
- In utero the anal and urinary openings start as one common channel, the cloaca; the urogenital septum then forms to divide the cloaca into the bladder in front and the anal canal behind. The disease arises when the urorectal septum from above fails to come down to join the cloacal membrane below → the upper part ends blind with no anus, or if there is an opening it is tiny, or it connects to the bladder or vagina.
- There are 3 types, too complicated for surgical residents: use the level of the rectal pouch — low type vs high type is enough, since intermediate type is treated like high type. The divider is the puborectalis muscle (the anal muscle that helps hold stool): rectal pouch ending below the muscle = low type; above it = high type.
- Most important: find where the fistula opening is, the level of the rectal pouch, and whether VACTERL syndrome is also present.
- Figure: Anorectal malformation classification —

| Level | Male | Female |
|---|---|---|
| High | Recto-vesical fistula; recto-prostatic urethral fistula | Recto-upper vaginal fistula |
| Intermediate | Recto-bulbar urethral fistula | Recto-lower vaginal fistula; recto-vestibular fistula |
| Low | Anocutaneous fistula | Anovestibular fistula; anocutaneous fistula |

- **Low type**: about 80%. No anus, or only a small perineal fistula; the skin is thick and bulging at the site where the anus should be.
  - Male: fistula opens on the perineum, median raphe and scrotum.
  - Female: fistula opens at the vulva and perineum.
- **High type**
  - Male: fistula opens into the urethra → urine has air (as printed "มีลม") or meconium; no black meconium staining the perineum as in low type. Remember: meconium in the urine = high type only.
  - Female: fistula usually opens into the vagina; meconium comes out of the vagina.
- **X-ray**: after ruling out serious associated anomalies, wait about 24 hours until air is clearly seen in the rectal pouch, then film.
  - Old method: Wangensteen-Rice invertogram — hold the baby head down with buttocks raised 45° to see air in the rectal pouch, to tell low from high type. This film is unsafe: with associated TE fistula (type with upper blind, lower connected), head-down lets gastric acid flow into the lung — disaster (ซวยเลย).
  - Now: baby prone, lateral x-ray (prone lateral cross table) — a roll beneath the hips elevates the buttocks so air migrates to the end of the rectum; see whether the rectal-pouch air lies above or below the pubococcygeal line (PC line).
    1. Low type: blind rectal pouch below the imaginary PC line, or < 1 cm from the anal skin (จำ)
    2. High type: pouch above the PC line, or > 1 cm from the anal skin
- Do not forget: in high type without fistula, x-ray may show no air if filmed too early; be patient, wait about 24 hours for air to reach the rectal pouch, then repeat the film.
- **Treatment** (การรักษา)
  1. Low type: perineal anoplasty without colostomy — operate through the buttock, pull down the rectal pouch and open it where the anus should be. How pediatric surgeons suture it is their business; residents should not take their work (as printed).
  2. High type: proximal sigmoid colostomy first; when the baby is 3 months old, distal colostogram to see the fistula position and the lowest level of the rectal pouch. Then pull the high rectal pouch down to make an anus — pediatric surgeons call it PSARP (posterior sagittal ano-recto plasty) — and close the colostomy later. Results may not be very good: 50% of children have incontinence.

## Biliary Atresia
- Incidence 1:20,000; F > M slightly.
- True cause unknown; hypothesized to be an abnormality of the bile duct and liver from the child's own immune problem with an inflammatory response, or viral infection injuring the bile duct, ending in fibrosclerosis of the duct.
- Bile duct, cystic duct and hepatic duct atrophy; GB atrophy only in 25%. Most cases involve both intra- and extrahepatic bile ducts. Atresia is classified into 3 types by location and extent — a general surgery resident cannot remember it; teacher will not blame you (as printed).
- Normal babies may have physiologic jaundice, but not beyond 2 wk.
- Babies with this disease are jaundiced (yellow skin and eyes) with pale/grey stool from birth, progressively; jaundice > 2 weeks that does not resolve; may have hepatosplenomegaly.
- Growth retardation, poor feeding; left > 2 months → hard liver, portal hypertension and splenomegaly.
- **Investigation**
  1. LFT: high direct bilirubin, or both fractions high, and lipoprotein X raised 20-40%
  2. Nuclear scan: in children DISIDA scan (like HIDA scan in adults) — Tc-labeled isotope + iminodiacetic acid (IDA), taken up by liver cells and excreted in bile; if isotope does not reach the duodenum within 24 hours → true bile duct obstruction
  3. Needle liver biopsy: periportal fibrosis (important), atrophy of intrahepatic bile ducts, bile duct proliferation and cirrhosis
  4. US: first investigation; a hyperechogenic area at the porta hepatis like an inverted triangle = triangular cord sign (the senior author says he cannot do it — as printed) → fibrosis of extrahepatic bile duct, and intrahepatic bile ducts all small, nowhere dilated. Some say if found, DISIDA is unnecessary — go to OR for IOC then Kasai.
  5. MRCP: nonvisualization of extrahepatic bile duct, atrophic gallbladder and periportal thickening
  6. Finally, if combined investigations still leave biliary atresia uncertain (e.g. DISIDA shows no excretion into the GI tract) → intraoperative cholangiography (IOC), open, laparoscopic or percutaneous: inject contrast into the GB to see bile duct patency; in this disease obliteration or atrophy of the bile ducts is seen. If IOC shows biliary atresia → Kasai straight away.
- **Kasai classification system**: biliary atresia in 3 types; the most common is type 3 (90%).
  - Type I: atresia of the common bile duct
  - Type IIa: atresia of the common hepatic duct
  - Type IIb: atresia of common bile duct, cystic duct, and common hepatic duct
  - Type III: atresia of the common bile duct, cystic duct, and hepatic ducts up to the porta hepatis — present in over 90% of patients with biliary atresia
- **Treatment** (การรักษา)
  1. Operate before the baby is 2 months old; otherwise the liver is usually hard and results are poor.
  2. Operation: Kasai operation or hepatic portoenterostomy — similar to what is done for cholangiocarcinoma: dissect at the porta hepatis, peel off the fibrous cord from the atretic GB and common hepatic duct up to the liver hilum between the bifurcation of the portal vein, then shave the fibrous tissue between the portal veins to leave a raw surface with small open bile canaliculi; cover it with a Roux-en-Y jejunum. Bile canaliculi are only about 100 micron, invisible to the naked eye — rely on the pathologist to confirm the dissection reached canaliculi, otherwise the cover is in the wrong place.
- After surgery, some institutes give steroid for about 5 years; if cholangitis occurs, give antibiotic episodically.
- Results of Kasai: 1/3 improve, 1/3 need OLT, 1/3 die.
- Recurrence after surgery → redo Kasai or go to OLT; if liver biopsy already shows cirrhosis → OLT.
- **Prognosis**: depends on age at diagnosis and timing of Kasai; after 3 months the liver is already damaged and prognosis is poor.
- Jaundiced newborns: also think of neonatal hepatitis. Remember liver biopsy can help distinguish it from biliary atresia, but not with 100% certainty.

## Omphalocele & Gastroschisis
- Abdominal wall defect, incidence 1:3,200-10,000 live births; 50% in first pregnancies or premature deliveries; may be associated with malrotation. Not hard to diagnose; both need surgery.

| | Omphalocele | Gastroschisis |
|---|---|---|
| Defect site | At the umbilical ring (centre of the umbilicus) | Right of the umbilical cord |
| Defect size (cm) | 2-15 cm | 2-3 cm |
| Amniotic sac covering | Yes | No |
| Umbilical cord | Lies on the sac | Left of the defect |
| Abdominal cavity size | Normal | Small |
| Bowel character | Normal | Swollen, inflamed (no sac covering) |
| Liver/spleen outside abdomen | Common | Not found |
| Bowel atresia | Rare | More common |
| Associated anomalies | 40-50% | < 10% |
| Mortality | 20-30% | < 10% |
| Treatment | Can wait | Emergency |

- Omphalocele mortality is much higher than gastroschisis because it carries many more serious associated anomalies.
- **Treatment of omphalocele** (การรักษา omphalocele)
  1. Cover the sac with NSS-soaked gauze or a transparent bowel bag; NG tube decompression.
  2. Do not rush to reduce the organs into the abdomen — the baby cannot breathe. If there are many anomalies (printed "anormalies") and the baby cannot tolerate surgery → conservative first: wait for escharization on the sac, i.e. epithelialization over the whole sac, until it becomes a ventral hernia; repair the ventral hernia later.
  3. Stable baby, no other anomaly needing urgent correction, defect ≤ 4 cm → primary closure. If the defect is wide and closure would be tight → silastic sheath or vicryl mesh as artificial sac coverage (silo) to cover the sac temporarily, then gently push the content in daily until the bowel returns into the abdomen; usually about 7-10 days, then primary closure is usually possible.
- **Gastroschisis** — complications:
  1. Hypothermia
  2. Infection
  3. Fluid electrolyte imbalance
  4. Bowel ischemia or gangrene
  5. Respiratory difficulty
  6. Abdominal compartment syndrome
- **Treatment of gastroschisis** (การรักษา gastroschisis): fairly emergency — cover the bowel first. Stable baby with a small defect → primary closure straight away. If not possible (very swollen bowel), forcing the bowel in compresses the IVC → reduced venous return to the heart, and pushes up the diaphragm → difficult breathing, possibly death. Recommended: silo — artificial sac coverage of low tissue-reaction material, e.g. silastic sheath, wrapped around the bowel and sutured to the defect edge; when the bowel shrinks, push it gradually into the abdomen and repair later.

## Patent Urachus
- In utero the urachus connects the urinary bladder with the abdominal wall at the umbilicus; normally after birth it closes, becoming the median umbilical ligament.
- If the urachus does not close: wet umbilicus, or urine may squirt from the umbilicus when the baby strains.
- Figure: Urachal anomalies — normal; patent urachus; urachal cyst; urachal sinus; diverticulum.
- If the orifice is closed → may present as urachal cyst.
- Problem: recurrent bladder infection/inflammation.
- Investigate before surgery: formerly fistulogram or voiding cystogram with lateral film; now US or CT to help diagnosis.
- **Treatment** (การรักษา)
  1. Excision via an infraumbilical incision: remove the urachal tract down to the bladder dome, then close the bladder opening → cure
  2. If abscess → drain first

## Cryptorchidism
- Undescended testis: incidence 1:200 or 3-5% of live births.
- Figure: Undescended testis positions — abdominal (15%), inguinal canal (25%), high scrotal (60%), normal.
- Normally the testis descends from the retroperitoneum into the scrotum at 7-8 months of gestation; if it does not, it usually sticks in the retroperitoneum, at the internal ring, external ring, or in the inguinal canal.
- Cancer risk increased 10-fold; bilateral undescended testes may cause infertility; higher chance of torsion, trauma or hernia.
- **Symptoms** (อาการ): only one testis palpable, or none. Bilateral disease is associated with hormonal abnormality; do a chromosome study.
- If not palpable in the groin → MRI may be needed to locate the testis.
- **Differentiate from**
  1. Retractile testis — testis has descended into the scrotal sac normally, but the cremasteric muscle contracts and pulls it up; when the muscle contracts the testis rises and when it relaxes it returns. No treatment needed; as the child grows and the testis enlarges it stays in the scrotal sac. Associated with indirect inguinal hernia.
  2. Ectopic testis — testis does not descend along the normal path but strays off to the thigh, abdominal wall or perineum.
- **Treatment** (การรักษา): formerly human chorionic gonadotropin (HCG) 1,500 unit every 3 days for 6 wk was recommended to bring the testis down; no longer recommended (very poor results). Once diagnosed, every case needs surgery.
  1. Testis palpable in the groin → bring it down into the scrotum (inguinal orchidopexy) to improve sperm production, reduce malignant degeneration (especially seminoma), and help the child's self-image.
  2. Intraabdominal type → 2-stage: locate the testis by US first, then laparoscopic exploration and clamp the testicular vessels; wait 6 months for good collateral circulation from the artery of the vas deferens, then orchidopexy.
  3. Operate at about age 1 year (ควรผ่าตัดตอนอายุขวบ): about 75% descend spontaneously by 3-6 months, so wait until age 1; if not down by then it usually will not descend. In summary < 1 year → observe; now surgery is done earlier, at 6-9 months. Waiting long makes the testis shrivel, like the teacher's (as printed, joke about อาจารย์ชาญวิทย์).
  4. Surgery after age 12, or testis looks abnormal → biopsy
  5. Age > 15 years → do not do orchidopexy; remove the testis (orchidectomy)
  6. Age 2-12 years → still do orchidopexy
  7. Age 12-15 years (borderline) → decide also by testis size

## Testicular Torsion
- The testis is loosely attached to surrounding tissue and twists around the axis of the spermatic cord → venous congestion and ischemia → atrophy or gangrene of the testis.
- Two age peaks at presentation: first year of life and 10-15 years.
- **Symptoms** (อาการ): sudden and severe pain; early on swelling and redness are minimal; the twisted testis lies high in a horizontal line; extremely tender to touch; hard to distinguish from orchitis.
- **Torsion**: pain rises quickly within 2-3 hours; lifting the testis makes the pain worse because lifting twists it more (-ve Prehn's sign); urine exam usually normal.
- **Orchitis**: symptoms develop over days; testis not high; supporting/lifting the testis eases the pain; urine exam may show infection.
- **Diagnosis**: if time allows and orchitis is hard to exclude, Doppler US — no blood flow to the testis (absence of perfusion on color Doppler). Testicular scan (delayed Tc-99m DTPA scan: absence of uptake in the testicle with increased flow to the scrotal skin) is unsuitable — takes about 24 hours; in true torsion the testis will be gangrenous first. So if Doppler is unavailable or uncertain but the clinical picture suggests torsion — high-riding testis, pain worse on lifting — explore the testis at once.
- **Treatment** (การรักษา): surgery within 6 hours → about 90% chance of saving the testis; vertical midline scrotal incision.
  1. Testis still viable → detorsion with orchidopexy on both sides, since the other side also has a high chance of twisting (Bell clapper deformity)
  2. Clearly gangrenous testis, usually twisted > 24 hr → orchidectomy
  3. Borderline → try warm water compresses; if gangrenous, remove it

## Abdominal Tumors
- Most common solid malignancy in the abdomen: neuroblastoma; by age:
  1. Age < 2 years → neuroblastoma
  2. Age > 2 years → Wilm tumor
- Most common liver tumor in children: hepatoblastoma.
- Most common abdominal tumor in girls: ovarian dermoid cyst.
- This chapter covers only Wilm tumor and neuroblastoma; hepatoblastoma is in the liver chapter.

## Wilm's Tumor
- Embryonal cell tumor of the kidney, also called nephroblastoma.
- Incidence 1:100,000; age 1-5 years, peak at 3 years.
- Mostly asymptomatic; usually presents with a palpable abdominal (frank) mass in the upper abdomen; the mass may be mobile; a left-sided mass may present with varicocele.
- 3-10% bilateral; 10% HT; 30% microhematuria.
- **Investigation**
  1. Plain KUB: calcification in 10%, at the rim
  2. IVP: distortion of the pelvis
  3. CT: Wilm mass replaces the parenchyma, whereas neuroblastoma displaces the kidney; Wilm mass does not cross to the opposite side, neuroblastoma can cross
  4. Biopsy not needed when the tumor is confined to the kidney
- **Spread** (การกระจาย): to lung and bone; neuroblastoma prefers the liver.
- **Treatment** (การรักษา)
  1. En bloc resection, removing the whole tumor with the kidney → cure 80%. Some cases need preop chemotherapy first, e.g. bilateral tumor, single remaining kidney, tumor invasion into the IVC above the hepatic veins, or heavy adherence to adjacent tissue/organs.
  2. After surgery, post-op chemotherapy and RT according to staging.
- Tumor confined to the kidney, not spread, completely resected → 4 year survival up to 97%. Even with hematogeneous metastases, adjuvant RT and chemo give 5 year survival of 80%.
- Local recurrence usually results from tumor rupture during surgery.

## Neuroblastoma
- From neural crest cells, origin of the sympathetic ANS, which normally migrate to the sympathetic chain and adrenal medulla.
- Usually in children < 2 years; F > M (as printed).
- Incidence 1:10,000.
- **Location**: most often the adrenal gland, then sympathetic ganglia anywhere, e.g. neck, posterior mediastinum and pelvis.
- Tumor encases vessels more than invading them.
- Most have metastasis already, especially liver, skin, BM, bone, skull, orbit.
- **Symptoms** (อาการ)
  1. 2/3 asymptomatic but with a palpable irregular mass; importantly the mass crosses the midline — different from Wilm
  2. HT uncommon, only 15%
  3. Some have secretory diarrhea from tumor secretion of vasoactive intestinal peptide stimulating peristalsis
  4. Some have bony metastasis
  4. (numbered as printed) Spread to the orbit → raccoon eyes
- **Investigation**
  1. Vanillyl mandelic acid (VMA), a metabolite of catecholamine (printed "catecholamime") made by sympathetic ganglia
  2. Film KUB: stipped (as printed) or scattered calcification 50%
  3. IVP: kidney displaced from above downward (now less used)
  4. US: kidney displacement; add CT — mass displaces the kidney and crosses the midline to the opposite side
  5. BM biopsy: cells arranged in a rosette pattern (ดอกกุหลาบ)
  6. MIBG scan
- Staging: residents need not know it in that detail; skip.
- **Treatment** (การรักษา)
  1. En bloc resection — cure 30-40%; preoperative preparation with BP control as for pheochromocytoma
  2. Adjuvant or neoadjuvant chemotherapy for large tumors that may not be completely resectable; results of chemo or RT are not as good as for Wilm
- **Prognosis**
  1. Depends on age at diagnosis: < 1 year overall survival 70%; > 1 year overall survival 35%. Remember: age is the most important factor.
  2. Crossing the midline → poor prognosis

## Sacrococcygeal Teratomas
- Teratoma: tumor arising from cells of all 3 germ layers — endoderm, mesoderm and ectoderm — which normally develop into skin, hair, cartilage, bone.
- 60-70% of teratomas are at the coccyx, usually as an exophytic mass; also found in testis, ovary, chest, retroperitoneum.
- Histopathology divides teratoma into 4 grades: mature (benign) — fortunately most are benign; a few are immature benign and immature malignant (precancerous lesion); and the frankly malignant grade, which can metastasize far.
- **Symptoms** (อาการ): weakness, paralysis, bowel or bladder dysfunction from intradural spinal extension.
- US, CT or MRI help assess pelvic or abdominal extension.
- Raised AFP and beta-hCG → beware yolk sac or choriocarcinoma components; about 15-20% undergo malignant transformation.
- **Treatment** (การรักษา) (numbered 1 then 3, as printed)
  1. Surgical resection together with removal of the coccyx; approach may be posterior, or also abdominal, depending on tumor extension
  3. Fetal surgery — operating while the baby is still in the mother's uterus

## Summary: Things to Remember (สรุป สิ่งที่ควรจำ)
- Bowel obstruction — consider age:
  1. Newborn to 1 month → think of Hirschsprung, duodenal and jejunoileal atresia, malrotation and midgut volvulus
  2. Age > 1 month to 2 years → think of intussusceptions, incarcerated hernia, Hirschsprung, HPS, malrotation
- Newborn with bilious vomiting is urgent, because if it is midgut volvulus you must act fast.
- **Duodenal obstruction**
  1. Newborn < 1 wk → duodenal atresia
  2. Age ~1 wk and overall → malrotation ? (question mark as printed)
- Most common colon obstruction: Hirschsprung.
- **UGI bleeding**
  1. Age 0-1 year → gastritis and esophagitis
  2. Age > 1 year to adolescence → esophageal varice
- **LGI bleeding**
  1. Painless → Meckel
  2. Painful (printed "painfull") → anal fissure
  3. Overall → anal fissure
  4. Children aged 2-8 years → also consider rectal polyp; bleeding is bloody mucus mixed with stool
