# Gastrointestinal Foreign Bodies and Bezoars 2020
Source: Surgical Board Review 2 (2563), นพ.พงษ์สันติ์ ทองเนียม · book pages 957-972 (PDF pages 961-976)

- Intro: odd things in the GI tract are a topic residents should know at a basic level; besides exams, residents at provincial hospitals may have to handle these cases themselves.

## Age & Incidence
1. Children: up to 80% of cases, from children being naughty or restless, inadvertent; the most common FB is a coin, next toys.
2. Adults: US figures show meat (e.g. steak) as the commonest, next construction workers who like holding nails, screws, batteries in the mouth.
3. Elderly: usually from accident; denture is the commonly found one.
- Also found in psychiatric patients, prisoners, and body packers who swallow drugs or insert them through the anus or vagina; most body packers use condoms; if the bag breaks there is a chance of death from drug overdose.
- 80-90% of FB can pass through the GI tract by themselves without severe injury, with only a little anal pain.
- 10-19% need endoscopic removal; only 1% need open surgical exploration.

## Indications for endoscopic or surgical removal
1. Complication, e.g. obstruction, bleeding, perforation, fistula.
2. Failure to progress through the GI tract; the time cut-off is usually 72 hr.
3. Associated with tumor or stricture.
- FB that passes the ligament of Treitz usually comes out by itself, because the small bowel contracts gradually pushing the FB down; once past the IC valve into the colon, it is easier, and the stool as the best bodyguard wraps the FB and carries it out through the anus.
- Currently, if possible, especially for FB stuck in the esophagus or stomach, endoscopic removal is preferred over waiting for it to be pushed down; the problem is whether to scope urgently or electively.
- Patients who should be scoped emergently: patients who swallowed sharp FB such as a needle, razor blade, chicken bone, or swallowed a disc battery, with difficulty swallowing even saliva, airway obstruction, or complications such as bleeding or obstruction.

## Sites where FB passes with difficulty
1. Esophagus: 1.1 UES, especially (75% in children stick here); 1.2 LES, found often in adults.
2. Pylorus. 3. Duodenal sweep or C loop. 4. Ligament of Treitz. 5. Ileocecal valve.
- Increased risk for obstruction: 1. FB longer than 5 cm or diameter over 2 cm; 2. a narrowed bowel with old stricture, e.g. pyloric obstruction or adhesion.
- Increased risk for perforation: 1. sharp or pointed FB; 2. alkaline battery with leakage.
- The site of perforation is usually at the duodenal sweep because it is a C loop.

## Working approach (Prof. Sawyer's advice)
Before treating an FB case, answer these questions:
1. Are we sure the patient swallowed a FB? If radio-opaque, no problem, send a plain film; but if rubber or plastic that is not seen on x-ray, what do we do?
2. What was swallowed: sharp or round, and is its chemical content dangerous?
3. Size, length, width: can it pass out through the anus?
4. If observing, how long is right; and where is the FB stuck now?
5. If the FB must be removed: use hand, scope, or open surgery.
- Diagnosis: from history and symptoms; there may be problems in child patients or psychiatric patients, who may not say much; if FB is stuck in the esophagus, the patient may say difficulty swallowing, painful swallowing, dysphonia, chest pain; some patients can feel where the FB is stuck, with about 30-40% accuracy; but if the FB has slipped into the stomach, symptoms are mostly absent; so in a case with painful swallowing that then disappears by itself, suggests the FB has probably slipped to the stomach.
- Imaging studies commonly used: plain film, CT and contrast study.
- Plain film chest and abdomen: should be the first study; easy, cheap, safe; also request a lateral view, to see whether the FB is in the esophagus or tracheobronchial tree; but only metallic contents are seen. Figure: abdominal film with FBs (skip).
- FB that may be hard to detect or not seen on plain film: 1. glass; 2. bone (chicken, fish); 3. aluminium; 4. plastic; 5. wood.
- In a case where plain film shows a clear FB in a position that can be removed, do flexible endoscopy to confirm and manage right away, without needing CT to confirm.
- CT scan: tells the position of FB precisely, sees both opaque and nonopaque FB, and if there is a complication such as perforation, it can be seen. Case figure: from plain film FB not seen, but CT sees a fish bone perforating the small bowel; open, remove the bone and suture the perforation.
- Thin barium or water soluble contrast study: when x-ray and scope do not see FB but we are still unsure that there is one, a contrast study helps, or in suspected esophageal perforation.

## Management
- First step: look at the airway for obstruction; if there is, treat urgently.
- Do not give laxatives or purgatives (memorize).
- Most patients are treated conservatively; but if a patient has fever, abdominal pain, guarding, indicating perforation or complete obstruction, it is an indication for open surgery to remove the FB.
- Antibiotic issue: if no perforation, no need to give; in those treated conservatively, admit and follow up closely, with abdominal examination, abdominal film and x-ray, which is enough.
- FB stuck at each site of the GI tract, and the kind of FB, are what we use to decide how to manage.

### Esophageal FB
- FB sticks most at the upper esophageal sphincter (UES). Figure: coin in children's neck films (skip).
- When diagnosed, should treat, since there are usually symptoms, and should do it before it slips down to the stomach.
- Endoscopic removal: seeing the FB clearly, use a grasper to pull it out; if the FB is sharp or has a pointed end, use the tube over scope technique.
- Foley catheter removal: patient lies head down, feet high, pass a Foley No. 12-16 into the esophagus with fluoroscopy as a helper; when the tip of the Foley passes the FB, inflate the balloon and pull the FB out carefully, because if it goes up to the upper airway the doctor is helpless, though trying to help the patient; this technique suits blunt FB, not sharp like a hook, no sharp edge, a single opaque piece; while doing so keep an ET tube and oxygen ready beside.
- Dilator: push the FB down into the stomach, then treat as gastric FB.

### Gastric FB
- If the FB is already in the stomach, the risk of complication is very low, and 90% pass out through the anus within 4-6 days.
- Consider the shape of the FB mainly:
  1. Observe and follow up, used with FB that has the following shape or features: 1.1 blunt or round; 1.2 length under 5 cm, likely to pass the duodenum; 1.3 diameter under 2.5 cm, likely to pass the pylorus. Observation should wait at most 2 weeks; if the FB does not progress, scope and pull the FB out; but if someone will pull it out right away, nothing wrong, as long as the person is experienced and there is no problem.
  2. Should remove if the FB is: 2.1 sharp or with a pointed end; 2.2 longer than 5 cm; 2.3 diameter over 2.5 cm.

### Small bowel FB
- FB in the small bowel is too far for the scope to pull out; must accept observation and follow up with frequent PE and abdominal x-ray every 3 days.
- Indication for exploration: 1. signs of peritonitis; 2. no progress or arrest at the same site for over 3 days (or 2 wk, per another statement) and still not out through the anus.
- If the FB passes the IC valve into the colon, mostly it all comes out.

### Colon FB
- As said, the stool in the colon wraps the FB and expels it through the anus; if help does not release it or it will not come out, use colonoscopy to help pull it out; if cannot pull, open and do colotomy + remove the FB.
- Except sharp FB that is hard, perforating, then must open the abdomen and treat as in trauma.

### Rectal FB
- Most occur from putting an FB into one's own anus for fun (pleasure): patients often say they slipped and fell in the bathroom or toilet onto a red crayon, long carrot, or red bottle placed on the floor that slid into the anus; it is so incredibly convenient [humorous].
- Figure: abdominal film with cylindrical FB (skip).
- FB in the rectum is of 2 kinds:
  1. Sexual FB: those with a taste for it use sex toys like vibrator or dildo; if poor (rural), or lacking funds, they use long carrot, eggplant, or red apple as aids; from self experience and case reports, items found include broken glass, kitchen knife blade; believed to be people who like pain and have a bit of bleeding, very sadistic.
  2. Body packing of illicit drug: put drugs in a condom and insert into the rectum or vagina.
- Symptoms: from minimal extraperitoneal mucosal injury to free perforation; certainly while the FB is stuck in the rectum there is a feeling of a mass wanting to defecate, the person feels full and tense to the point of misery; some have rectal bleeding.
- Physical examination: if severe pain, abdomen rigid, x-ray with free air suggests intraperitoneal perforation; must open the abdomen right away.
- Management: remember: do not give enema or wash the rectum to push the FB out, because enemas make the bowel swell and perforate more easily.
- Ways to remove a rectal FB depend on where it is stuck:
  1. Low lying FB: FB still in the rectal ampulla; at this position usually the FB can be removed through the anus:
     - 1.1 First step: try in the ER or OPD first by injecting marcaine mixed with adrenaline 1:100,000 about 10 cc into 4 quadrants, to reduce pain and help relax the anal sphincter.
     - 1.2 Then have the patient try bearing down (Valsalva maneuver), or cheer as in the labor room; if it still does not come out, try adding a bit of light sedation, put in an anoscope, try pulling out and squeezing the buttock, help by consulting; if still not out, it is advised to send to the OR and do a spinal block.
     - Remember: the first step is to try transanal removal under local anesthesia.
  2. High lying FB: FB at the rectosigmoid or above; for this, must go to the OR straight away, with spinal anesthesia or general anesthesia, patient in lithotomy position; then help by gently pressing the lower abdomen; if the FB does not move, remains stuck, may need scope help, or may need open explore, going in to try squeezing the rectum to help, then try pulling from the lower side again; if still not out, open the bowel and take the FB out.
- For body packing drug cases: do not use a scope; use forceps to pull, because if the condom ruptures there is rapid drug absorption, even death; to remove the group of drugs stuffed in the rectum, use a finger via PR, pull gently, wrapping the condom containing the drug out carefully; do not use force.
- After pulling the FB out of the rectum, do postextraction endoscopy to see how much mucosal injury of the rectum there is: if only bruised mucosa or hematoma, nothing needs to be done; but if a tear is seen, which is usually from an FB that was hard to pull out, observe in hospital first; if there are no signs suggesting perforation, let the patient eat; then advise the patient that the anus is a path for stool to leave and must not be used for the wrong purpose.

### Blunt or small FB
- Not sharp, not pointed, diameter under 2.5 cm, length under 5 cm.
- Example of such an FB: coin.
- If in the esophagus or stomach, if it can be pulled out, pull it out at once; but if it has gone into the small bowel properly, can observe comfortably; follow with x-ray to see progression of the FB at least every 3-7 days.

### Sharp, pointed or long FB
- Sharp, pointed, longer than 5 cm, e.g. toothpick, nail, sewing needle, chicken bone, razor blade, safety pins, toothbrush, denture; especially toothpick and chicken bone often perforate near the terminal ileum.
- For this type of FB, if it is still in the stomach: remove with flexible endoscopy through an overtube; but if the FB has passed the stomach into the small bowel, be conservative and observe, because even a nail or glass fragment can often be passed out; just thinking about it is hair-raising.
- Normal pulling out of a sharp or pointed FB may scratch the mucosa until it tears; for safety, use an overtube or hood to cover first, then pull out gently. Figure: overtube and latex hood (skip).
- Indication for surgical removal: 1. perforation or obstruction; 2. fails to reach the colon within 3 days.

### Button or disc batteries
- Look like a small coin, containing alkaline solution, which if it leaks out causes liquefaction necrosis. Figure: battery sizes and chest film (skip).
- If size over 21 mm there is a high chance of problems.
- On average about 10% have symptoms.
- Treatment: the principle is to remove it quickly, because of the risk of caustic injury to the esophagus and stomach.
  1. Stuck in the esophagus or stomach: have the scope remove it at once; but do not induce vomiting, because if aspirated into the trachea it becomes a bigger problem. Figure: battery grasped by scope (skip).
  2. If it has passed the stomach into the small bowel, wait and watch: examine often, x-ray the abdomen every 1-3 days; in general within 72 hr the battery will be passed out in the stool.
  3. Signs of peritonitis: must explore.
- For AA and AAA alkaline batteries in the esophagus and stomach, scope them out too; but once past the pylorus, they usually pass out through the anus without complication.

## Illicit Drug
- Drug smugglers (body packers, also called mules) like to put heroin, cocaine or amphetamine into multiple small packages, usually condoms stuffed into the anus; swallowing is limited since they can only hold so many; besides, their stuff has a double layer of packing and rubber, so it can pass through the whole tract.
  - So they usually swallow condom-packed drugs, and to keep them in the body a long time they often take antidiarrheal drug to stop vomiting and loose stool, waiting to pass them out at the appointed place. Figure: abdominal film with packets (skip).
- If few packets are swallowed there may be no symptoms; in cases with problems it is because they swallowed many; 80-90% are seen as condom packages on plain film; the more sensitive one is CT abdomen.
- Treatment:
  1. Conservative: observation and serial x-ray at intervals, waiting for it to pass out.
  2. Urgent surgical removal: 2.1 package ruptures: inject antidote then hurry to operate; 2.2 swallowed so many that there is obstruction or failure to progress through the GI tract.
- In the opinion of Schwartz 9th: look at the severe part: a bag that ruptures has such a toxic effect that it kills; so surgery is advised over conservative treatment, because if it ruptures the patient mostly dies from drug toxicity.
- The point everyone agrees on: do not remove drug packets through the endoscope, because when clamping and pulling the bag, it may rupture, with rapid absorption of the drug, which can kill the patient in the scope room (memorize this).

## Bezoars
- The name comes from Persian meaning "against poison".
- Definition: retained collections of nondigestible material in the GI tract; found in any site, but the one we meet most is in the stomach.
- Predisposing factors for bezoar formation:
  1. Previous TV+A or TV+P (vagotomy with drainage), which lowers acid and slows gastric emptying.
  2. Gastroparesis. 3. DM. 4. End stage renal disease. 5. Psychiatric disease.
- Classification:
  1. Trichobezoars: bezoars from fibers of hair, wool, cotton, clothing.
  2. Phytobezoars: bezoars from vegetable, e.g. persimmon, palm fruit, [fruits like] fak thong (pumpkin).
  3. Pharmacobezoars: found with drugs: nifedipine, vitamin C, vitamin B12, ferrous sulphate, ASA, theophylline.
  4. Lactobezoars.
- Imaging studies: plain film, barium swallow, CT.
- Upper endoscopy: the gold standard for diagnosis and also helps treatment, pulling it out through the scope.

### Trichobezoars
- Bezoars from hair or hair-like fiber, which resists gastric acid and cannot be digested; as hair is swallowed repeatedly, it packs tight until gastric outlet obstruction occurs. Figure: operative photo of large hair bezoar (skip).
- Trichomania: those with underlying psychological abnormality who like eating hair (neurotic).
- Trichophagia: a habit of shyness: when teased by LINE or embarrassed, they pull hair to play with and then swallow it.
- Found often in: 1. children; 2. younger women (in the past, young girls, when teased by young men until shy, they pulled grass, bit the hem of clothing, pulled hair then swallowed; red faces, so cute, shy for days and nights; but in this age, men who are builders [as printed, a humorous aside]); 3. mental retardation; 4. psychiatric disorder.
- Such patients have sparse, patchy hair, and may palpate a movable mass in the upper abdomen.
- Upper GI series: shows an intragastric mass.
- Gastroscope: sees a mass of hair strands in the stomach lumen, rather hard like concrete.
- Rapunzel syndrome: a trichobezoar filling the stomach and extending down to obstruct the 2nd part of the duodenum, causing jaundice and pancreatitis.
- Treatment of bezoars is summarized below together.

### Phytobezoars
- Phyton is Greek for plant, meaning bezoars of vegetable origin. Figure: phytobezoar in the small bowel (skip).
- Usually found in patients who have had TV+A or TV+P before, or have a problem of impaired gastric emptying, e.g. DM.
- Gastroscope: bezoars are brown mixed with green or black.

### Pharmacobezoars
- Found in oral drugs that are resin coated to dissolve slowly so the effect lasts longer; but sometimes the drug does not dissolve and sticks together as a mass, a strange thing stuck in the bowel.
- Gastroscope: sees whole pills or pill fragments (figure, skip).

### Lactobezoars
- A group of undigested milk stuck together as a mass, usually found in infants.
- Plain film: seen as white mass in milk with calcium mixed in the upper abdomen.
- US upper abdomen: hyperechoic mass. Figures: lactobezoar on endoscopy, plain film, US (skip).

## Treatment of Bezoars
- Aim: 1. bezoar removal; 2. prevention of recurrence.
- Conservative: suits small bezoars: may put in an NG tube and lavage or give clear liquid together with prokinetic drug; in the lactobezoar group, stop milk, and slowly do gentle gastric lavage until the bezoar dissolves completely.
- Endoscopic treatment: the most used method; can be used with all kinds of bezoar, success rate 85-90%; the principle is to try fragmenting the bezoar into small pieces and slowly pulling them out through the scope; may need repeated treatment several times until all is gone; tools for fragmenting: biopsy forceps or polypectomy snares, but now there are more high-tech helpers, e.g. electrohydraulic lithotripsy (EHL), pulsed water jet, Nd:YAG laser, needle knife.
- Enzymatic dissolution: rarely used; suits phytobezoars; in the text, Coca-Cola is given, believing the gas bubbles will soften the bezoar, but the author thinks Pepsi or any other fizzy drink may work too; give cellulase to help digest vegetable; but if animal meat is mixed in, advise meat tenderizer or tenderizing powder, which is a substance in the papain group.
- Surgical intervention: indications: 1. failure of endoscopic treatment; 2. complications such as obstruction, perforation, bleeding.
  - The bezoar that needs surgery most is the trichobezoar: if it stays long in the stomach, it clumps hard as a stone, so fragments cannot be pulled out by the scope. Figure: lap explore, trichobezoar removal.
  - After taking the bezoar out of the stomach, run the small bowel too; if a bezoar is in the small bowel, two ways: enterotomy and remove the bezoar, or push it down into the colon to let it come out.
